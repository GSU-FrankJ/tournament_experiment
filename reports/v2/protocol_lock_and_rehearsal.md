- a new mode `locked` (flags must be expected / frozen / stage1_rows / mean; fixed budget);

**Result: the check fails on its literal "all RNG states" criterion. Three process-global RNG states differ in 20/20 runs: torch global, numpy legacy global, python `random`. Everything training uses is bit-identical in 20/20:**
- actor, critic, lagged opponent, both Adam states;

- the torch generator;
- all 64 weight exports from u25 to u1600;
- the stage-2 metrics at u1600.

**Why the three global states differ.**
- The runner never seeds them. A fresh process initializes them from OS entropy; in this environment `torch.initial_seed()` is also entropy-derived (17167615931904387274 in one fresh process).
- The full state records them, and `restore` copies them. The stitched path therefore carries Pilot 1's process-start values, while each rehearsal process carries its own.
- **Training never consumes them:**
  - In the stitched path all three are byte-identical at u400 (Pilot 1 end), u1200 (extension) and u1600 (2a). They were restored and never advanced over 1,200 updates.
  - In the rehearsal they are identical at the end of A and the end of B of the same run, and different between runs (seeds 10501 and 10502).
  - The numpy legacy state is still at position 623, the position right after seeding.
- If any of them were used, the exports and the RNG streams would diverge. They do not.

So the training state is reproduced exactly; the mismatch is a property of the recorded diagnostics. **Your instruction was to stop on a Check 1 failure and explain.** I did not change any code, the protocol or the check, and I ran nothing new afterwards. The rest of this report only analyses runs that had already completed: the 20 rehearsal runs, the 2 Check-2 runs and the cusp fits all finished before the check was evaluated.
