The Tournament Game

Two players choose effort levels e₁, e₂. A noisy comparison determines the winner: the higher eᵢ + εᵢ wins prize w_H, the loser gets w_L. Noise εᵢ ~ Uniform[-q, q] controls how random the outcome is.

Lazear & Rosen (1981) showed the unique symmetric Nash equilibrium is:

e*(q) = (wH − wL) / (4 · q · k)

The question: can a model-free RL agent rediscover this from experience alone?

Component 01
Beta Policy Parameterization
The agent outputs parameters α and β for a Beta distribution over normalized effort [0, 1], scaled to the effort bounds [0, 200]. As training proceeds, the distribution sharpens around the equilibrium effort.
Fig 1. Evolution of Beta distribution parameters α and β during training. The policy concentrates as the agent converges toward equilibrium.
Component 02
Cheap-Gate Convergence Detection
KL divergence between consecutive policies is monitored over a sliding window. Exploitability checks (expensive) trigger only when mean_kl < 0.0045 and std_kl < 0.0035—saving computation while ensuring checks happen at policy stability.
Fig 2. Approximate KL divergence per update. The cheap-gate thresholds (dashed) determine when exploitability evaluation is triggered.
Component 03
Exploitability Evaluation
Given the current policy, a best-response is computed analytically. If the best-responder gains less than ε = 0.05 over the agent's strategy, the policy is at Nash equilibrium. This must hold for patience = 5 consecutive checks.
Fig 3. Exploitability score over training. Values near zero confirm the agent's strategy is a Nash equilibrium.
